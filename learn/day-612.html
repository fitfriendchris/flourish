Appoint Elders

Day 612 of 1,095

The reason I left you in Crete was that you would set in order what was unfinished and appoint elders in every town, as I directed you. An elder must be blameless, the husband of but one wife, having children who are believers and who are not open to accusation of indiscretion or insubordination. As God’s steward, an overseer must be above reproach—not self-willed, not quick-tempered, not given to drunkenness, not violent, not greedy for money. Instead, he must be hospitable, a lover of good, self-controlled, upright, holy, and disciplined. He must hold firmly to the faithful word as it was taught, so that he can encourage others by sound teaching and refute those who contradict it.

Titus 1:5-9 · Berean Standard Bible

For men

Paul gives Titus a leadership standard before he gives him a strategy. The church in Crete needs elders whose private lives can withstand scrutiny, because the community's purity depends on leaders who are not secretly compromised.

For women

Paul's standard for elders is also a standard for the households they lead. A woman benefits from knowing what trustworthy leadership looks like, and she helps form it by the honesty and prayer she brings to her church.
